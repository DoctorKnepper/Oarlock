Use when someone is unresponsive and not breathing, or only gasping. Call emergency services and get an AED if one is nearby.

Hands-only CPR (adults and teens)

  1. Lay the person on their back on a firm surface.
  2. Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), the other hand on top.
  3. With straight arms, push hard and fast: at least 5 cm (2 inches) deep, but not more than 6 cm (2.4 in), at 100–120 compressions per minute.
  4. Let the chest come all the way back up after each push.
  5. Don't stop until help takes over, an AED tells you to, or the person starts breathing.

If you are trained and willing, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions.

AED (automated external defibrillator)

Turn it on and follow its spoken instructions. Attach the pads to bare skin as pictured on them. Make sure nobody touches the person when it analyses or shocks. Resume CPR right after.

Children and infants

For children, push about 5 cm (2 in) deep, using one or two hands. For infants, use two fingers in the center of the chest, about 4 cm (1.5 in) deep. Rescue breaths are especially important for children and drowning victims.
