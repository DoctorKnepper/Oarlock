Burns

  1. Stop the burning: remove the person from the source, and take off jewellery and clothing near the burn unless stuck to the skin.
  2. Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter or ointments.
  3. Cover loosely with plastic cling film or a clean non-fluffy cloth.
  4. Seek medical help for burns larger than the person's palm, on the face, hands, feet, genitals or joints, deep burns, chemical or electrical burns, and any burn on a young child or elderly person.

Shock

Signs: pale, cold, clammy skin; fast, weak pulse; fast breathing; confusion; thirst. Treat the cause (such as bleeding), lay the person down, raise the legs if no injury prevents it, keep them warm, and give nothing to eat or drink. Get help urgently.

Fractures and sprains

Keep the injured part still in the position found. Support it with padding, a sling or a splint (a rigid object tied above and below the injury, not over it). Check that fingers or toes beyond the splint stay warm and pink. Do not try to straighten a badly deformed limb unless trained.

Head, neck or back injury

If a fall, crash or blow might have injured the spine, keep the person still and support the head in line with the body. Move them only to save them from immediate danger.
