Hypothermia

Signs: shivering (which may stop as it worsens), slurred speech, clumsiness, confusion, drowsiness.

  • Get the person out of wind, rain and cold, and off the ground.
  • Replace wet clothing with dry layers. Wrap them, including the head, in blankets or a sleeping bag; share body heat if needed.
  • Give warm, sweet drinks only if fully alert. No alcohol.
  • Handle severe hypothermia gently — rough movement can trigger heart problems.

Frostbite

Skin turns white or grayish, hard and numb. Don't rub it. Rewarm in warm (not hot) water only if it won't refreeze. Protect blisters and get medical care.

Heat exhaustion

Heavy sweating, headache, dizziness, nausea, cramps. Move to shade, loosen clothing, cool with wet cloths and fanning, and sip water or an electrolyte drink.

Heat stroke — an emergency

Very high body temperature, hot skin (dry or sweating), confusion, collapse or seizures. Call for help and cool the person immediately: immerse in cold water if possible, or soak with water and fan, and put ice packs on the neck, armpits and groin. Do not give drinks to someone who is confused or unconscious.
