Life-threatening bleeding can kill within minutes. Act immediately and call for help.

Direct pressure

  1. Expose the wound so you can see where the blood is coming from.
  2. Press firmly on it with a clean cloth, gauze or your hand. Use both hands and lean your weight onto it.
  3. Keep pressing without lifting to check. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top and keep pressing.

Wound packing (deep wounds in the groin, armpit, neck or a limb)

Stuff gauze or clean cloth tightly into the wound, right down to where the bleeding is, until it is full. Then hold firm pressure on top for at least 3 minutes.

Tourniquet (arm or leg)

  1. Place it 5–7 cm (2–3 inches) above the wound, never on a joint.
  2. Tighten until the bleeding stops. It will hurt; that is expected.
  3. Note the time it was applied and tell emergency responders. Do not loosen it yourself.

An improvised tourniquet needs a wide band (at least 4 cm / 1.5 in, such as a folded triangular bandage) and a stick to twist it tight. Thin cords and wire cause damage and don't work well.
