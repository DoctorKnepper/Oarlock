This guide is a quick reminder, not a substitute for first-aid training. If emergency services can be reached, call them first.

1. Make sure the scene is safe

Before helping anyone, look for fire, traffic, downed power lines, unstable structures, gas smells, floodwater or violence. You cannot help if you become a second casualty.

2. Call for help

  • US, Canada and Mexico: 911. Most of Europe: 112. UK: 999 or 112.
  • Text-to-911 works in many US areas when a call won't connect.
  • Give your location first, then what happened and how many people are hurt.

3. Check the injured person

Treat the most life-threatening problems first:

  1. Massive bleeding — stop it (see Severe Bleeding).
  2. Airway — is it open? Tilt the head back and lift the chin if there's no neck injury.
  3. Breathing — if not breathing normally, start CPR.
  4. Circulation — look for signs of shock: pale, cold, clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, confusion.
  5. Warmth — keep the person off cold ground and covered.

The rule of threes

A rough guide to survival priorities: a person can survive about 3 minutes without air, 3 hours exposed in harsh weather, 3 days without water and 3 weeks without food. Deal with them in that order.
